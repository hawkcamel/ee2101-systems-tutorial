LESSON 8 / FROM FOURIER TO LAPLACE

Build a signal from sinusoids.

Before calculating transforms, let us see what a change of description can reveal.

1. Build a waveform by adding waves

A sinusoid has one frequency. A more complicated waveform can contain several. Start with the first wave below, then add the third and fifth harmonics. A harmonic has an integer multiple of the fundamental frequency; here the fundamental is 1 Hz.

Individual sinusoids

Their sum

Teal is 1 Hz, orange is 3 Hz and purple is 5 Hz. Changing one amplitude changes the whole sum. Changing phase moves just that component in time. The square-wave preset uses the first three odd harmonics. It rounds the corners and overshoots near the jumps; a finite sum cannot reproduce a sharp jump exactly.

2. From a list of harmonics to a spectrum

For a repeating signal, a Fourier series gives a list of harmonic amplitudes and phases. For a non-repeating signal, the Fourier transform describes a continuous range of frequencies. Both let us ask the same practical question: which oscillations make up this signal?

The complex exponential tests one frequency at a time. Its real and imaginary parts compare the signal with a cosine and a sine. A large magnitude means a strong contribution at that frequency. The angle records phase. This ordinary integral requires convergence; some signals, such as an everlasting sinusoid, need a more general interpretation.

3. What Laplace adds

Some signals decay, others persist or grow. Laplace adds an adjustable exponential weight before making the frequency comparison. Write s = σ + jω and separate the two factors:

For each fixed σ, this is the Fourier transform of the weighted signal. At σ = 0, it reduces to the Fourier transform of x(t), provided the imaginary axis lies in the region of convergence. In this sense the bilateral Laplace transform generalises the Fourier transform. In the next lesson we work with causal signals, which are zero before t = 0, so the integral starts at zero.

Try x(t) = eᵃᵗu(t). Change a to choose a decaying or growing signal. Then move σ until the weighted signal decays. The plots show a finite time window; the convergence test concerns all t ≥ 0.
