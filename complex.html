LESSON 02 / COMPLEX NUMBERS

Complex numbers, made visible.

A short detour: distance, direction, multiplication and division. These are the tools behind the pole–zero map.

One point, several notations

A complex number q = x + jy is a point and a vector from the origin. Its magnitude r is its distance from the origin. Its angle θ, also called its argument or phase, is measured counterclockwise from the positive real axis.

The function atan2 uses both coordinates to choose the correct quadrant for the angle. For example, 1 + j has angle 45°, whereas −1 − j has angle −135°.

Move the dot or change the coordinates. At zero, the magnitude is zero but its argument is undefined. Angles differing by 360° represent the same direction.

What does s − z₁ look like?

Mark s and z₁ on the complex plane. Draw an arrow starting at z₁ and ending at s. To find its horizontal and vertical components, subtract the coordinates of z₁ from those of s. This new vector represents s − z₁.

The length r₁ of this new vector is the distance from z₁ to s. Its angle θ₁ is measured counterclockwise from a horizontal line pointing to the right. For example, if s = 1 + 2j and z₁ = −1, then s − z₁ = 2 + 2j: move two units right and two units up.

Drag either point, or use the sliders. The dashed orange arrow is the same vector moved to the origin: its components and length do not change. The complex number s − z₁ contains both length and angle; its magnitude is the distance.

Purple solid arrow: starts at z₁ and ends at s. Its length is the distance |s − z₁|.

Orange dashed arrow: starts at the origin and ends at the complex number s − z₁. It is a translated copy of the purple arrow: same length, same angle, different starting point. Moving a vector without rotating or stretching it does not change the complex number it represents.

The grey dashed lines show the horizontal and vertical coordinate differences. If z₁ is at the origin, the orange and purple arrows coincide. If s = z₁, both arrows have zero length.

Multiply lengths; add angles

When multiplying complex numbers, multiply their magnitudes and add their angles (phases). When dividing, divide their magnitudes and subtract the denominator’s phase from the numerator’s phase. The result is a new complex number plotted from the origin. Try both operations below: the left plot shows factors as vectors ending at s; the right shows their product or quotient.
