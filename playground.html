    4 · The poles and zeros behind it

    Each × is a pole and each ○ is a zero, in rad/s (Lessons 9–10). Watch them while you drag sliders:

    • In the RLC circuits, lowering R moves the two poles towards the jω axis. The Bode peak gets sharper and a step makes the output ring for longer.
    • The notch's zeros sit right on the jω axis. That's why one frequency is completely blocked.
    • High-pass circuits have zeros at the origin. A zero at s = 0 means DC can't get through.

    5 · Challenges

    These tick themselves off when your circuit meets the target.

    • First-order circuits (one L or C) have one corner and roll off at 20 dB/decade.
    • Second-order circuits (L and C) can resonate. Q, set by R, controls how sharp the peak is and how long they ring.
    • The Bode plot predicts the settled sine wave. Sharp edges in a square wave contain high frequencies, which is why filters round them off or turn them into spikes.
