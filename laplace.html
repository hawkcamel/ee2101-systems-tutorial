What does the transform calculate?

Here the signal starts at t = 0 and is zero before then. This is what we mean by a causal signal. The exponential weight has a decaying part and an oscillating part:

The transform adds the weighted signal over time. It exists only where that integral converges—the region of convergence (ROC). You may still be able to substitute other values of s into the formula, but those values do not come from a convergent transform integral.

Change the signal in either domain

A transform pair describes the same signal in two forms. For an exponential, the connection is particularly direct:

The pole is at s = a. A negative a gives decay; a positive a gives growth. For an oscillating signal, the real part still sets the envelope, while the imaginary part sets the oscillation frequency.

Time domain: change the waveform

For oscillations, “envelope” means the upper and lower curves that bound the signal. A ratio of 0.5 means the envelope halves each second; a ratio of 1 means it stays constant.

Three comparisons to try
  1. Choose an exponential. Move the pole from −1 to −2. Compare the values at t = 1 s. Then move it to +0.5.
  2. Choose a sine. Keep the real part fixed and double the imaginary part. The period halves, while the envelope is unchanged. Now change the period using the time-domain control and watch the poles move.
  3. Compare a step, a ramp, and a delayed step. All have a pole at zero, but their formulas and time signals differ. Check the pole’s multiplicity and the numerator.

Moving a pole changes X(s) and x(t). Moving an evaluation point s in a fixed X(s) only changes the value being measured. The integral experiment below explores that second operation.

Close the loop: impulse, step, frequency

For a stable system in this tutorial, we can set s = jω in H(s) to find the gain and phase of its settled sine-wave response. The transform integral must converge on this imaginary axis. If the system’s own response keeps growing or never dies away, we cannot assume that the output will settle to the sine wave predicted by H(jω).
