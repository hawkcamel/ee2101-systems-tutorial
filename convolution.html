LESSON 06 / CONVOLUTION

Convolution: add the responses.

Each impulse produces a shifted copy of h(t). Add those copies to predict the output.

Start with three impulses

Use h(t) = e−tu(t). The three input arrows below denote impulse areas. Change their weights and watch their individual responses combine.

The sum of the responses

Now let the input be continuous

The star denotes convolution, not pointwise multiplication. For causal signals and a system initially at rest, the integral can be restricted to 0 ≤ τ ≤ t.

To evaluate one output value: hold t fixed, shift the reversed impulse response to h(t − τ), multiply it by x(τ), and integrate over τ. Then move t and repeat.

Slide, multiply, accumulate

At this t: the integrand

Gray: x(τ). Purple: h(t − τ). Teal shaded region: x(τ)h(t − τ). Horizontal axis is τ, not t.

Accumulated output

The purple dot is the area under the product at the selected t. The teal curve repeats this calculation for every t.

Check against an exact result

The area is nonnegative for these two nonnegative inputs and this nonnegative h. Convolution need not be positive for signals with negative values.
