    How do we add up all the kicks?

    Convolution: adding up the echoes.

    Every kick in the input leaves a trail behind it. The output is all of those trails stacked on top of each other.

    Clap once in a big empty hall and you hear the clap followed by a tail of echoes. That tail is the hall's impulse response. Now talk in the hall: every syllable gets its own tail, and what reaches your ear is all those tails piled up. That pile-up is convolution. Music apps use exactly this to make a voice sound as if it were recorded in a cathedral. They convolve it with a recorded clap.

    • add the responses to three separate kicks by hand
    • read the convolution integral as "flip, slide, multiply, add"
    • find one output value as the area under a product curve

    Step 1Start with just three kicks

    Use the same circuit as before: h(t) = e⁻ᵗu(t). The input is three kicks, at t = 0, 1 and 2 s. Change their sizes and watch each one's echo, and their total.

    Add the three echoes

    1. Look at the output after t = 2 with the starting values. It is almost flat at zero. Why? Every echo has the same shape, e⁻ᵗ, so once the last kick has happened the echoes can cancel exactly. Try −0.39 for the third kick.
    2. Set all three to 1. The output keeps climbing while the kicks keep coming. You're starting to see a step response.

    Step 2Infinitely many kicks: the integral

    Read it slowly. To get the output at one time t, we look back at every earlier moment τ. The input there, x(τ), is a kick. That kick's echo, measured now, is h(t − τ), because t − τ seconds have passed since it happened. Multiply and add up over all τ.

    Flip, slide, multiply, add. Flip h back to front, slide it so it ends at time t, multiply it point by point with x, and find the area under the product. That area is y(t). Move t and do it again.

    If the input starts at t = 0 and the circuit starts at rest, nothing happens before 0 and nothing after t matters yet, so the limits shrink:

    Two different times live in this formula. t is "when are we asking about the output?" and stays fixed during one integral. τ is "when did each kick happen?" and runs over the integral. The star ∗ means convolution, not multiplication.

    Step 3Slide, multiply, add: see it happen

    The input is a 2-second pulse. When will the output be at its largest: during the pulse, exactly when the pulse ends, or after it ends?

    Check your guess

    Exactly at t = 2 s, when the pulse ends. Up to then the capacitor keeps charging. After that the input is zero and it can only discharge. Slide t to 2 and see where the teal area is biggest.

    At this t: what gets multiplied

    Grey: x(τ). Purple: the flipped, slid echo h(t − τ). Teal shading: their product. Its area is y(t). The horizontal axis is τ, not t.

    The output, built up point by point

    The purple dot is the teal area on the left. The teal curve is that area calculated for every t.

    Check against the exact answer

    The first is the RC step response from Lesson 4. We've now derived it a second way. The pulse answer is "step response, minus the same step response two seconds later", which is linearity and time-invariance at work.
