LESSON 05 / BUILDING WITH IMPULSES

Build a signal from impulses.

The ideal impulse is a mathematical tool for describing an input concentrated at one instant.

A spike with an area, not an ordinary height

Imagine making a pulse narrower while keeping its area equal to 1. Its height must increase. The ideal limit is the unit impulse, written δ(t). We draw it as an arrow and label its area, not its height. The equations above say that its total area is 1 and that an impulse at t₀ picks out f(t₀) when we integrate.

As the pulse narrows, its height grows while its area stays one. The plot shows an ordinary pulse approaching that ideal. Mathematically, δ is called a distribution: it is defined by what it does inside an integral, rather than by an ordinary height at each point.

A table becomes a weighted combination

Take f(t) = exp(−t²/4). Sample it from −5 to 5. Refine the spacing to see more samples and smaller weights.

The impulse at τₖ has area f(τₖ)Δτ, not f(τₖ) alone. Each area represents a thin slice under the curve. The spikes do not trace the curve’s height; their areas approximate its contribution inside an integral. Smaller spacing gives more, smaller contributions. Our table stops at −5 and 5, leaving out the small tails beyond those times.

One impulse reveals an LTI system

The output to a unit impulse, starting from rest, is h(t): the impulse response. Time invariance gives the response to a delayed impulse; linearity gives the response to a scaled impulse and to sums.
